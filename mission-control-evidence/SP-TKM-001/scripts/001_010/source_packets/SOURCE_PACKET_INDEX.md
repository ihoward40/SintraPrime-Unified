## Detailed Packets

### UCC001 — What a UCC-1 Actually Does

- **Proposition:** Filing a UCC-1 financing statement is a public notice step that can perfect a security interest; it does not by itself create the underlying security interest.
- **Source title:** Uniform Commercial Code Article 9 (Secured Transactions)
- **Authority type:** Primary (uniform act, adopted with variations by states)
- **Jurisdiction:** U.S. states and territories (state-specific versions)
- **Limitations:** State filing-office rules vary; exact perfection requirements vary for certain collateral.
- **Recommended wording:** "A UCC-1 filing puts the public on notice. It does not create the security interest. You need an agreement and collateral first."
- **Citation link:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9 (general reference; verify state version before production)

### UCC002 — UCC Filing vs. Enforceable Security Interest

- **Proposition:** A filed UCC-1 alone does not mean the secured party has an enforceable interest in specific collateral.
- **Source title:** UCC § 9-203 (attachment and enforceability)
- **Authority type:** Primary
- **Jurisdiction:** U.S. states (variations exist)
- **Limitations:** Attachment requires value, rights in collateral, and authenticated security agreement (with exceptions).
- **Recommended wording:** "Filing and enforceability are two different things. Ask whether the interest attached to actual collateral."
- **Citation link:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-203

### UCC003 — Why Collateral Must Exist

- **Proposition:** A security interest must attach to identifiable collateral.
- **Source title:** UCC § 9-203
- **Authority type:** Primary
- **Jurisdiction:** U.S. states
- **Limitations:** Collateral description requirements vary; after-acquired property clauses have special rules.
- **Recommended wording:** "You cannot have a security interest in nothing. The collateral has to be described and exist."
- **Citation link:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-203

### DEBT001 — Debt Validation vs. Debt Cancellation

- **Proposition:** A consumer's right to request validation of a debt under the FDCPA is not a right to cancellation.
- **Source title:** Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1692g
- **Authority type:** Primary federal statute
- **Jurisdiction:** United States
- **Limitations:** Applies to debt collectors; original creditors may not be covered in the same way.
- **Recommended wording:** "Validation means the collector has to show you certain information. It does not erase a valid debt."
- **Citation link:** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/policies/fair-debt-collection-practices-act-text

### DEBT002 — What a Charge-Off Means

- **Proposition:** A charge-off is generally an accounting recognition that a debt is unlikely to be collected; it does not eliminate the debt or legal obligation.
- **Source title:** IRS and CFPB materials on charge-offs
- **Authority type:** Secondary / agency guidance
- **Jurisdiction:** United States
- **Limitations:** State law and contract terms govern continued collection after charge-off.
- **Recommended wording:** "Charged off is an accounting term. The debt may still be owed and may still be collected."
- **Citation link:** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-charge-off-en-1453/

### DEBT003 — What Assignment Evidence May Look Like

- **Proposition:** A party attempting to collect an assigned debt may need to show chain of assignment; what is sufficient depends on jurisdiction and court.
- **Source title:** State contract assignment law, court rules of evidence
- **Authority type:** Jurisdiction-dependent
- **Jurisdiction:** Varies by state and court
- **Limitations:** This is not a universal checklist; production should not imply a specific document always wins.
- **Recommended wording:** "What proves an assignment depends on where the case is and what the court requires. This is a general overview."
- **Citation link:** None universal; state-specific research required before any court-content script.

### EVID001 — How to Build a Debt Timeline

- **Proposition:** Organizing records chronologically helps identify gaps, contradictions, and deadlines.
- **Source title:** General evidence-organization principle
- **Authority type:** Practical / educational
- **Jurisdiction:** Universal
- **Limitations:** A timeline is a tool, not proof of any legal conclusion.
- **Recommended wording:** "Put every date, letter, and payment on a timeline. Gaps and contradictions become visible."
- **Citation link:** N/A

### EVID002 — Five Documents to Preserve

- **Proposition:** Common records in consumer disputes include contracts, statements, letters, credit reports, and payment records.
- **Source title:** General consumer-education practice
- **Authority type:** Practical / educational
- **Jurisdiction:** Universal
- **Limitations:** Specific cases may require additional or different documents.
- **Recommended wording:** "Most disputes benefit from five kinds of records: the agreement, statements, letters, credit reports, and proof of payment."
- **Citation link:** N/A

### MAIL001 — Why Mailing Proof Matters

- **Proposition:** Retaining proof of mailing helps document communications and deadlines.
- **Source title:** USPS Certified Mail and general evidence principles
- **Authority type:** Practical / educational
- **Jurisdiction:** Universal
- **Limitations:** Proof of mailing does not prove receipt, legal effect, or winning a dispute.
- **Recommended wording:** "Keep your mailing receipts. They show when and how you sent something."
- **Citation link:** https://www.usps.com/ship/certified-mail.htm

### MAIL002 — What Certified Mail Does Not Prove

- **Proposition:** Certified mail proves mailing and attempted delivery; it does not prove legal conclusions, receipt by the right person, or that a deadline was met in all cases.
- **Source title:** USPS Certified Mail definitions; general evidence principles
- **Authority type:** Primary / practical
- **Jurisdiction:** United States for USPS rules; legal effect varies by case
- **Limitations:** Some rules (e.g., court deadlines) may require specific service methods.
- **Recommended wording:** "Certified mail proves you mailed it. It does not prove the law is on your side."
- **Citation link:** https://www.usps.com/ship/certified-mail.htm
