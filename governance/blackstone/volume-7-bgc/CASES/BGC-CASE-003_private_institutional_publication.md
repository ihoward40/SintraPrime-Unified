# Case — Private Institutional Publication

## Case ID

BGC-CASE-003

## Title

Private Institutional Publication

## Scenario

A private research foundation publishes a detailed monograph on a historical trust arrangement. A user asks whether the monograph can be used as authority in a brief about trust administration.

## Inputs

- Private foundation monograph (scholarly, peer-reviewed within the foundation).
- Governmental trust statute.
- Leading commercial treatise on trusts.

## Governing Principles and Articles

- BKGC-R-003 Source Neutrality
- BKGC § V — Constitutional Research Doctrine
- BKC-C-004 Authority
- BGS-S-007 Source Classification
- BKR-TERM-006 Authority

## Evidence Evaluation

| Evidence | Source Category | Weight | Notes |
|----------|-----------------|--------|-------|
| Trust statute | Government Publication | Controlling | Governs trust administration within jurisdiction |
| Commercial treatise | Commercial Publication | Persuasive | Widely cited by courts |
| Foundation monograph | Private Institutional | Persuasive / Scholarly | Evaluated by methodology, not origin alone |

## Reasoning

1. The statute is the controlling authority.
2. The commercial treatise is persuasive because courts respect it.
3. The private foundation monograph is not rejected because it is private. It is evaluated by its methodology, corroboration, and transparency.
4. If the monograph's analysis is well-supported and consistent with statutory text, it may be cited as persuasive or scholarly authority.

## Outcome

- Use the statute as controlling authority.
- Cite the commercial treatise as persuasive.
- Include the foundation monograph as scholarly support if its methodology is sound.
- Disclose that the monograph is a private institutional source.

## Lessons Learned

- Private and minority sources are legitimate research inputs when evaluated by evidence quality.
- Origin alone must never determine acceptance or rejection.
