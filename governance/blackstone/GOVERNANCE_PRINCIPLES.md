# Blackstone Governance Library — Principles

## Bill of Rights for Governed Knowledge

These principles apply to every volume, engine, agent, and implementation in the Blackstone ecosystem. They are intentionally brief, durable, and ranked.

1. **Evidence over assumption.**
   No claim shall be treated as governed until it is supported by evidence that can be identified, examined, and reproduced.

2. **Provenance over convenience.**
   The origin and custody of evidence shall be preserved even when doing so is slower or more difficult than omitting it.

3. **Transparency over certainty.**
   It is better to disclose uncertainty than to present an unsupported conclusion as settled.

4. **Revision over dogmatism.**
   Conclusions may be revised when materially better evidence becomes available. The prior reasoning shall be preserved.

5. **Reproducibility over anecdote.**
   A governed conclusion must be reachable by another reviewer who starts from the same evidence and applies the same process.

6. **Methodology over ideology.**
   Sources shall be evaluated by their evidence quality, provenance, and methodological soundness, not by their political, institutional, or popular status.

7. **Jurisdiction over generalization.**
   Legal conclusions shall be governed by the controlling authority of the relevant jurisdiction, not by broader or inapplicable authority.

8. **Counter-evidence over suppression.**
   Material contrary evidence shall be identified and preserved, not omitted to strengthen a preferred conclusion.

9. **Auditability over opacity.**
   Every significant decision, recommendation, and conclusion shall be traceable to evidence, reasoning, and reviewer identity.

10. **Human accountability over automation.**
    AI agents may assist governance; humans remain accountable for consequential decisions.

## Application

These principles do not dictate conclusions. They dictate the process by which conclusions become governed. They are the foundation for the Articles of the Blackstone Knowledge Governance Constitution.
