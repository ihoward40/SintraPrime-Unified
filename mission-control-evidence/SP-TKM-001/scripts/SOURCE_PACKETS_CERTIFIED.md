## Detailed Packets

### UCC001 — What a UCC-1 Actually Does

- **Legal proposition:** Filing a UCC-1 financing statement is a public notice step that can perfect a security interest; it does not by itself create the underlying security interest.
- **Governing jurisdiction:** U.S. states and territories (state-specific versions of the Uniform Commercial Code Article 9)
- **Authority level:** V2 — supported secondary interpretation referencing primary authority
- **Primary authority:** UCC Article 9, § 9-310 (perfection of security interests by filing), § 9-203 (attachment and enforceability)
- **Date checked:** 2026-07-27
- **Exceptions:** State filing-office rules and collateral types vary; exact perfection requirements differ for certain collateral.
- **Statements that must not be made:** "Filing a UCC-1 creates a security interest." "A UCC-1 gives you a lien." "Filing alone makes you a secured party."
- **Safe plain-language wording:** "A UCC-1 filing puts the public on notice. It does not create the security interest. You need an agreement and collateral first."
- **Citation link:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9

### UCC002 — UCC Filing vs. Enforceable Security Interest

- **Legal proposition:** A filed UCC-1 alone does not mean the secured party has an enforceable interest in specific collateral.
- **Governing jurisdiction:** U.S. states (state-specific UCC)
- **Authority level:** V2
- **Primary authority:** UCC § 9-203 (attachment and enforceability)
- **Date checked:** 2026-07-27
- **Exceptions:** Attachment requires value, rights in collateral, and authenticated security agreement, subject to exceptions.
- **Statements that must not be made:** "A filed UCC-1 means the debt is enforceable." "Filing perfects everything."
- **Safe plain-language wording:** "Filing and enforceability are two different things. Ask whether the interest attached to actual collateral."
- **Citation link:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-203

### UCC003 — Why Collateral Must Exist

### DEBT001 — Debt Validation vs. Debt Cancellation

- **Legal proposition:** A consumer's right to request validation of a debt under the FDCPA is not a right to cancellation.
- **Governing jurisdiction:** United States
- **Authority level:** V1
- **Primary authority:** Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1692g
- **Date checked:** 2026-07-27
- **Exceptions:** Applies to debt collectors; original creditors may not be covered in the same way.
- **Statements that must not be made:** "Validation erases the debt." "If they do not validate, you owe nothing."
- **Safe plain-language wording:** "Validation means the collector has to show you certain information. It does not erase a valid debt."
- **Citation link:** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/policies/fair-debt-collection-practices-act-text

### DEBT002 — What a Charge-Off Means

- **Legal proposition:** A charge-off is generally an accounting recognition that a debt is unlikely to be collected; it does not eliminate the debt or legal obligation.
- **Governing jurisdiction:** United States
- **Authority level:** V2
- **Primary/secondary authority:** CFPB consumer guidance; IRS materials
- **Date checked:** 2026-07-27
- **Exceptions:** State law and contract terms govern continued collection after charge-off.
- **Statements that must not be made:** "Charged off means the debt is gone." "You no longer owe a charged-off debt."
- **Safe plain-language wording:** "Charged off is an accounting term. The debt may still be owed and may still be collected."
- **Citation link:** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-charge-off-en-1453/
