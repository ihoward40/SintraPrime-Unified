## UCC001 — What a UCC-1 Actually Does

- **Script ID:** UCC001
- **Content Pillar:** Myth correction
- **Target Viewer:** Consumer who has heard that filing a UCC-1 creates rights or "claims" status
- **Primary Question:** Does filing a UCC-1 create a security interest?
- **Hook (0–3s):** "Filing a UCC-1 does not create the debt. It puts people on notice."
- **Problem (3–15s):** Some posts say a UCC-1 filing gives you a security interest over someone else. That is not what the filing does.
- **Rule or Principle (15–40s):** A UCC-1 financing statement is public notice. It can perfect a security interest that already exists through a separate agreement. The filing itself does not create the underlying right.
- **Example (40–60s):** Think of it like recording a mortgage. Recording does not create the loan. The loan and the lien come first. Recording tells the world the interest exists.
- **Limit or Exception (60–70s):** State filing-office rules and collateral types vary. What is required in one state may differ in another.
- **Action Step (70–80s):** If you see a UCC-1, ask: was there an agreement? Was there collateral? Did the interest attach?

## UCC002 — UCC Filing vs. Enforceable Security Interest

- **Script ID:** UCC002
- **Content Pillar:** Myth correction
- **Target Viewer:** Consumer confused about whether a filed UCC-1 means a lender has an enforceable claim
- **Primary Question:** What is the difference between a filing and a security interest?
- **Hook (0–3s):** "A filed UCC-1 is not the same as an enforceable security interest."
- **Problem (3–15s):** People see a filing and assume the secured party wins. Filing is only one step.
- **Rule or Principle (15–40s):** For a security interest to be enforceable, it generally must attach to collateral through an agreement, value given, and rights in the collateral. A filing perfects notice; it does not fix a missing attachment.
- **Example (40–60s):** A store files a UCC-1 for a refrigerator you never financed. The filing exists, but if there is no valid security agreement and no collateral rights, the filing does not make the claim enforceable.
- **Limit or Exception (60–70s):** Courts decide enforceability based on the full record, not just the filing.
- **Action Step (70–80s):** Request the underlying security agreement and a description of the collateral.

## UCC003 — Why Collateral Must Exist

- **Script ID:** UCC003
- **Content Pillar:** Consumer-law fundamentals
- **Target Viewer:** Consumer trying to understand secured transactions
- **Primary Question:** Why does a security interest need actual collateral?
- **Hook (0–3s):** "You cannot have a security interest in nothing."
- **Problem (3–15s):** Some theories claim a security interest exists without identified collateral. That is not how the rule works.
- **Rule or Principle (15–40s):** A security interest must attach to identifiable collateral. There must be value, rights in the collateral, and an authenticated security agreement, with limited exceptions.
- **Example (40–60s):** If a document says a lender has a security interest in "all your property" with no specific collateral listed, that description may not be enough.
- **Limit or Exception (60–70s):** Collateral descriptions have special rules for after-acquired property and certain asset classes. State law controls the details.

## DEBT001 — Debt Validation vs. Debt Cancellation

- **Script ID:** DEBT001
- **Content Pillar:** Myth correction
- **Target Viewer:** Consumer who believes requesting debt validation will erase a debt
- **Primary Question:** Does validating a debt make it go away?
- **Hook (0–3s):** "Debt validation is not debt cancellation."
- **Problem (3–15s):** Some posts imply that if a collector cannot validate within thirty days, the debt disappears. That is not what the law says.
- **Rule or Principle (15–40s):** Under the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, a debt collector must provide certain validation information, and a consumer may dispute the debt. Validation helps you verify what is owed and who is collecting. It does not automatically cancel a valid debt.
- **Example (40–60s):** You ask a collector to validate. They send the required information. The debt still exists, but now you know the basis for the claim.
- **Limit or Exception (60–70s):** The FDCPA applies to debt collectors. Original creditors may not have the same validation duties. State laws may add rights.
- **Action Step (70–80s):** Use validation to gather records, then compare the claim against your own documents.

## DEBT002 — What a Charge-Off Means

- **Script ID:** DEBT002
- **Content Pillar:** Document literacy
- **Target Viewer:** Consumer who sees "charged off" on a credit report or statement
- **Primary Question:** Is a charged-off debt cancelled?
- **Hook (0–3s):** "Charged off is an accounting term. It is not a magic eraser."
- **Problem (3–15s):** Many people think charge-off means the debt is gone. It usually means the creditor wrote it off for accounting purposes.
- **Rule or Principle (15–40s):** A charge-off is generally a creditor's recognition that a debt is unlikely to be collected. The underlying obligation may still exist and may still be collected, sold, or reported.
- **Example (40–60s):** Your credit card account is charged off. The creditor or a buyer may still send collection letters or file suit within the applicable time limits.
- **Limit or Exception (60–70s):** State law and the original contract determine whether collection can continue and for how long.
