# CDR-0003 — Adopt Governed Knowledge Doctrine

## Problem

AI systems can produce fluent, confident, and wrong outputs. The ecosystem needs a way to distinguish trustworthy conclusions from persuasive ones without appealing to popularity or authority alone.

## Alternatives Considered

1. **Truth-seeking standard.** Rejected because "truth" invites irresolvable philosophical disputes and implies certainty.
2. **Governed-knowledge standard.** Selected because it focuses on process, evidence, review, and revision.

## Decision

The ecosystem produces governed knowledge, not Truth. A claim becomes trustworthy when it has been produced, evaluated, preserved, and reviewed according to the constitutional process. The Constitution governs how knowledge claims are collected, evaluated, documented, challenged, revised, and communicated.

## Governing Principles

- BKGC § 3.1 Governed Knowledge Doctrine
- BKGC § 3.2 Knowledge Claims and Process

## Expected Consequences

- Claims are evaluated by evidence and process, not by how widely they are believed.
- Revision is expected and documented rather than treated as failure.
- The ecosystem avoids debates about absolute truth while remaining rigorous.

## Revisit Conditions

Revisit only if the governed-knowledge standard proves insufficient for litigation or scientific contexts; in that case, extend the standard rather than replace it.
